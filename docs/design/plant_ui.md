## 1. What the plant is, and the rules it keeps

The plant is the lab's main place: a walkable 3D floor on which the synthetic run of one seed plays as motion. Two bays of bioreactors (24 reactors in two bays of 12, A001; N by M adjustable, ADR-0009, ADR-0015), the analyzers (2 by default, A022), the staff of the roster, the sample path from the draw to the analyzer, and one record hall per data architecture that shows what that architecture stored. The reader is an operator: walks the floor, looks, picks an object to see what a record holds, and acts through a bottom bar. The five S3 screens stay as drill-downs that objects open (section 7). The plant makes no claim about a real plant and predicts nothing.

| Rule | What it means in the plant |
|---|---|
| Truth and recorded world stay separate (invariant 1) | Three layers (section 3.3): the floor (the plant as it happened, the simulation's own view), the truth overlay (the hidden facts, only on request) and the record halls (only what each architecture stored). The page imports no engine module (`lab_ui.md` section 2). The Worker sends a scene plan, a projection of the truth run onto drawn things with every hidden field and every truth-only event type removed, never a log (section 4.1); section 6 states this as an amendment of rule 1 of `lab_ui.md` section 4. A record hall never shows the absence of a row; absence appears only in the truth overlay (section 3.3). |
| Determinism (invariant 2) | The scene is a pure function of the scene plan and the clock (section 4.2); no `Math.random` in `src` (the grep of `check_all.sh` covers it): decor such as dust and sway comes from a fixed integer sequence. Wall time moves the clock and nothing else. An operator action is plain data keyed by the canonical tuple, and the run it makes is recomputed from its start (section 5). The reader's avatar is a view object: its position is in no request and changes no result. |
| Every number carries an id or a result label (invariant 3) | The three classes of `lab_ui.md` section 6 hold in every panel. Text inside the 3D scene is ids and labels only (sample, unit, analyzer, batch), drawn as DOM overlays that follow their object, never as canvas text, so no font file ships and the Korean texts are ordinary DOM. The clock stamp (`day 8 08:31`) and the speed (`x120`) are labels. A figure in a panel is `<Ref>` or `<ResultBlock>`. |
| No result before it exists (invariant 9) | The plant shows one seed as a replay under the inputs shown, with the footer notice of every screen ("synthetic, not a prediction of a real plant"). It has no score, no ranking and no text that says which architecture wins; its feedback is the visible consequence of an action. |
| Headless browsers only (invariant 7); no publishing (invariant 6) | The end-to-end checks of section 10 run headless Chromium through Python Playwright and close it. Nothing here publishes anything. |
| Nothing copied from another product (ADR-0021) | Section 2. Every model, texture and sound is made for this project or carries a recorded licence the project can ship. |

**Assets.** Version one is procedural: geometry built in code from primitives, the floor and label textures drawn on a canvas, sound from oscillators. ## 3. The scene

### 3.1 The layout comes from data

`layoutOf(layoutSpec)` is a pure function (no three.js, unit-tested in Node) that returns positions in metres for everything the scene holds. Its input is the shape of the run, nothing else: the bays (`{ size, profile }` per bay, from N, the bay size and `bay_profiles`; ADR-0015), the analyzers (`{ profile, group }` each, from M and `analyzer_profiles`), the break-out items when the scenario has a `breakout` block (lots and equipment), and the architectures of the record halls.

- Bays: the number of bays is N over the bay size, rounded up (N from 5 to 100, A001); a bay is a plinth with its vessels in rows of at most six (12 vessels: two rows of six, as the spike drew it) and a bay plate; bays are placed in rows of at most three with an aisle between rows and columns, so that for N = 100 the floor stays within 100 m on both axes (a layout test checks the bounds, an expected value typed by hand).
- Analyzers: M from 1 to 6 (A022), in a row at a central bench, each with its intake position; the intake lane (the queue tray) is shared by analyzers of one group, as the engine's queue is (`request_queued` carries a `group`).
- A walk graph: the aisles as nodes and edges, from which the avatar's click-to-walk path, the staff's walks and the tube's carry path come. Every vessel, analyzer, shelf and object of interest is reachable on it (a layout test: connectivity for N from 5 to 100 and M from 1 to 6).
- As built in P3 (`src/plant/layout.ts`, the metres in one table `METRES`, every expected value of its test typed by hand from them): the floor from the north is the record halls (a wall of shelves 5 m long and 1.2 m deep for each architecture, on a pitch of 6 m, in rows that wrap when the working width is too short for them), the bays (a plinth 14 m by 6 m for a bay of 12; a vessel every 2 m, a margin of 1 m; three bays to a row) and the bench (1.2 m deep, 2.4 m for each analyzer and 1.2 m at each end), with an aisle of 3 m between every two bands and a wall margin of 2 m. The aisles' middle lines are the walk graph: the outer aisles run the full depth, the aisles between bay columns along the bays only, so no edge crosses an obstacle (a test). The access point of a vessel is on the aisle south of its bay, in front of it; of an analyzer, on the aisle in front of the bench; of a hall, on the aisle in front of it. The default scenario's floor is 41 m by 24.4 m; N = 100 in bays of 12 is 58 m by 42.4 m. The entrance is on the aisle in front of the bench, in the middle of the floor, so that the avatar and the camera behind it look north across the bench and the bays to the halls. A vessel's profile changes its drawn shape and never its position.
- Profiles change the look and nothing else: the bioreactor profile picks the vessel class (bench vessel, mini-parallel block, pilot tank: `bench_manual`, `micro_mini_parallel`, `pilot`), the analyzer profile the analyzer model (all-in-one box, chromatography unit with a tray, the split pair); each profile's grade chip comes from the ledger view as on Setup (A065 to A073). A mixed fleet shows mixed bays.

### 3.2 Objects, and how each is drawn

Shape and text carry the meaning; colour only repeats it (ADR-0011, `lab_ui.md` section 8). Repeated meshes are instanced.

| Object | Drawn as | Shows (from the scene plan) | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactor | A vessel per profile with a status lamp: idle, running, fed (a pulse), alarm, stopped, harvested; a tag with the batch id on proximity | Its marks at the clock instant (section 4.3) | Reactor panel (batch, status, the newest samples, a trend strip of its recorded process values) |
| Sampling board (one per bay) | A board listing the day's plan: unit and planned time | `samples[].planned_s` of the day | The target of `move_draw` (section 5) |
| Staff | A figure per rostered operator with an id tag, on duty or off at the clock instant | Walks from the bench to the reactor of a draw and back | Staff panel (the operator id, draws so far, the qualification as the lens record holds it; the truth overlay adds the truth-side status) |

*As built in P5b* (the panels and the pick; `src/plant/panels.ts`, `Panels.tsx`, `pick.ts`, `gl/plant_scene.ts`):

- **A panel is a function of the plan and the instant.** `panelOf(plan, pick, t, lens)` is pure (no three.js, no clock); `Panels.tsx` draws what it returns, so the numbers of a panel are the numbers the plan gives at the clock instant, and the end-to-end check computes the same ones from the plan. Every figure in a panel is a label, every time a clock reading (`day 8 08:31`). A panel is a region with its heading focused when it opens; `Esc` or its button closes it and the focus goes back to the button that opened it (the record panel's rule). A panel shows what the table above lists; its links to the S3 screens are those of section 7 (P5c).
- **Reactor.** Its bay, profile and batch (none before its inoculation), the status at the instant (the four words of the vessel lamp), when it was inoculated and, once over, when it ended, how many samples were drawn on it so far and the newest five, each a button that picks that sample. The trend strip of its recorded process values waits (section 12, item 13): no existing answer carries those values, and `plant_record` holds the rows of one sample, not a series.
- **Sample.** Its unit and operator and the label method as the floor has them (the plan), its phase now (in the hand, on the carrier, in the lane, in the analyzer, away, or not yet drawn), the times the floor has (planned, drawn, arrived) and each visit (analyzer, attempt, queued, started, run started, ended, QC outcome), and, from `plant_record` of the lens hall, the fields of its sample row as that record holds them (`not stored` where it stores none; the panel reads the record only while the lens hall has a card of the sample at the instant, and says so when it has not, without saying why). Buttons: follow (section 3.4) and open the record (the card's record panel, which shows the rows and, with the overlay on, the truth).
- **Analyzer.** Its profile and group, its lamp and the sample inside, the intake lane of its group (the samples waiting in queue order: the front first), the visits it has finished by the instant (a count) with the newest five, and its windows (the ones open at the instant, and the next one). No metric: utilisation belongs to Results.
- **Staff.** The operator id, whether on duty at the instant and what the figure is doing (resting, walking, at a vessel), the draws so far (a count) and the newest five, and the qualification as the lens record holds it: stored when the lens hall has `audit_trail` on (the record keeps the reference of the qualification in force with each draw), otherwise `not stored`. The reference itself is in no answer the page can ask for, so it is not shown. The truth-side status waits (section 12, item 12).
- **Picking.** A click picks the nearest object along the ray among the vessels (the cylinder of its profile, as a box), the analyzers (their bodies), the staff on duty (a box about a figure), the tubes (a box about a tube, larger than the tube so that a 0.1 m tube can be hit) and the cards (the arithmetic of P5a, on the front face of a hall, compared by the distance along the ray with the others). `pickNearest` of `pick.ts` is pure: a ray and boxes in, the nearest box out; a unit test puts one object behind another on the ray. A click that meets none walks the avatar, as before. The objects list gains the staff, and a list of the tubes on the floor at the instant (the newest twelve, as the cards list does) is the keyboard path to the sample panel.

*As built in P6a* (the sampling board: `src/plant/board.ts`, `BoardList.tsx`, `gl/boards.ts`):

- **What is on a board.** `boardsAt(plan, t)` is pure: for each bay of the layout, the samples of the plan whose planned instant falls on the day of the clock instant and that are not drawn yet at it (`drawn_s` null or later than `t`; a draw skipped by the volume budget leaves the board at the instant the operator reached the vessel, as a drawn one does), in order of the planned instant and then the sample, each with its unit and its planned instant. A board is the day's plan, so it empties as the day goes and fills at midnight; the plan holds every sample of the run (394 in the default one) and a board that listed them all would be a table. A row whose planned instant the clock has passed (the sample is late, A063) stays until it is drawn, and a `move_draw` of it is the Worker's `past` to refuse: the page does not hide a row the rule would refuse, because one rule has one place (section 5.1).
- **The target of `move_draw`.** The form of the action lists the rows of the boards of the run, grouped by bay; a button of the objects list ("Look at B1") brings the camera to a board and opens the form on that bay. Picking a row sets the sample, and the new instant `to` is a time of day on the day of the row: the form never offers another day, and the Worker refuses a time outside the staffed window as `outside_window`.
- **In the scene.** A board 1.6 m wide and 0.9 m tall on two posts at the west end of the north edge of each bay's plinth (view constants, `BOARD` in `gl/boards.ts`; the look is the author's to adjust) wears its id (`B1`, `B2`, ...) as a label and, when the camera is within 16 m, the first four rows as `U03 12:50` labels and the count of the rest (ids and clock readings, DOM labels as the scene's others are). It counts in `data-plant-objects` as `board`. The objects list has a fourth group, a board for each bay, and the text alternative a table of the rows of each board at the clock instant.

*As built in P6b* (the flags board: `src/plant/flags_board.ts`, `gl/flags.ts`, `ObjectsList.tsx`, `TextAlternative.tsx`, `ActionForm.tsx`):

- **What is on it.** `flagMatrix(plan, flags)` is pure: a row for each flag of `ready.flags` (in its order) and a column for each hall of `plan.layout.architectures` (in the order of the layout), each cell on or off as that hall's own record has it (`flags_on` of the record of the plan). A hall whose record has not arrived yet (the others follow `plant_ready`, section 5.5) has cells that are neither: they are drawn as small flat marks and read "not yet" in the text. A hall derived by a `set_flag` shows its own flags, not those of the record it was made from: the plan names it by the derived id (`c_context_minus_<flag>`) and its record is the one the Worker built from the overridden document.
- **In the scene.** One panel on the north wall, centred, its top at 2.95 m (view constants in `flags_board.ts`; the look is the author's to adjust): the flags down the left as DOM labels (ids, as the scene's others are, shown within 16 m), the id of each hall above its column, and a cell for each (flag, hall), an instance of one mesh: raised and blue when the flag is on, flat and pale when it is off, a small flat mark when the record has not arrived (shape carries it, colour repeats it). `focusOf` brings the camera to it, at the middle of the columns of cells and at a high pitch (0.65 rad), so that the middle of the view meets the sixth row of the middle hall, because the halls stand in front of the wall and hide its lower part from a low camera. It counts in `data-plant-objects` as `flags` (one).
- **The cell is the surface of `set_flag`.** A click on a cell (a ray against the plane of the panel, `cellAt` of `flags_board.ts`, compared by distance along the ray with the cards and the other objects; a ray that enters a hall, a bay or the bench before the wall is aimed at that, since the panel stands behind them) and a cell button of the objects list open the flag form of P6a on that hall and that flag, with the opposite of the cell's state; the camera is brought to the panel. Nothing is sent until the reader applies it (section 5.1), and a flag that already has that state in the run is the Worker's to answer, not the page's.
- **The target is the record the run began with.** A `set_flag` names the record of the spec (`c_context`), never the derived id the plan shows it by, because the Worker looks the record up among the architectures of the request (section 5.3: two switches on one record give one derived id). The flag form, and so a cell, take the base id from `spec.run.architectures` at the position of the hall and show the hall by the id the plan has for it. Found in P6b: the form of P6a offered the derived ids of `plan.layout.architectures`, so a second switch on a hall that the first had derived was refused as `unknown_target`; the form now takes the base ids (a test with a derived hall).
- **The lists.** The objects list has a group of its own with the board as one object (a button that brings the camera to it) and, under it, a table of the matrix whose cells are buttons (`data-flag-cell`; the cells are not objects, so `data-plant-objects` counts the board once). The text alternative has the same matrix as a table of words ("on", "off", "not yet"), which is the whole board when WebGL is missing; the matrix belongs to the run and not to an instant, so it does not follow the clock.

### 3.3 Three layers: the floor, the truth overlay, the record halls

| Layer | What it is | Built from | Visible | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floor | The plant as it happened: the true instants and places of the physical events (draws, carries, queues, runs, feeds, alarms), every hidden field removed | The scene plan (section 4.1) | Always | "Floor: the simulation's own view" in the HUD |
| Truth overlay | What the simulation knows and no record can hold: for a picked sample its truth column and verdicts (the unit, the true `sampled_at`, the true process context; each field of each record correct, wrong or unanswerable) and the errors the injector materialised on it (`lab_ui.md` section 8); in a break-out scene the hidden onset, origin and affected units; in a record hall a dashed placeholder for a row the plant produced that this record does not hold | A `plant_truth` answer (section 6), made by the existing lineage and break-out functions; no new kind of truth reaches the page | Off by default; a switch "Show what the simulation knows"; refused while a challenge is open (`locked`) | "Simulation's own view, not available to any record" on every overlay element |
| Record halls | What each architecture stored, and when | The recorded datasets of that architecture (`RecordedDataset` of the lab functions), as cards in the scene plan | One hall per architecture; the HUD's lens chip picks the one the panels read, the others dim | The architecture id and its flags on, on the hall's sign |

A card is the three questions of the lineage view, with the result, made an object: the unit key, `sampled_at`, the process context (with the join basis, `sampled_at` or `measured_at`, as a dashed tether to the physical sample) and the result value. A slot is solid when the record stores the field and dashed and hollow ("not stored") when it does not; the view never guesses a value. A card appears at the earliest instant the record holds anything of the sample. The rule found in P2: a sample row carries no write stamp in any architecture, so that instant is the first of the sample's run rows (`started_at`, stamped by `run_started`) and its result rows (`ingested_at` where the system stores it, otherwise `measured_at`); a sample with no run and no result has no card. The card turns "knowable" at the earliest `available_at` where the architecture stores it (null where it does not). The context slot is stored when the record can join a process context to the sample, with the basis `sampled_at` where the record stores that time and `measured_at` where it does not (as built in P5a: the join is the one of the lineage view, `recordContext`, which reads the first result the record offers, so a sample whose results are all rejected has no context slot, and the record panel shows no process-context box for it). Pennants mark the record's own validation findings (rule id) and, in a break-out scene, holds and re-flags. A superseded version is a stack behind the card where the architecture keeps versions (`append_only_versions`); where it does not, the card is replaced in place and no stack exists. A card is never marked wrong inside a hall, because "wrong" needs truth; the verdict appears only in the truth overlay.

*As built in P5a* (`src/plant/halls_at.ts`, `hall_shelves.ts`, `card_look.ts`, `gl/motion_halls.ts`, `gl/shelves.ts`, `RecordPanel.tsx`, `CardList.tsx`; the metres are view constants, `SHELF` of `hall_shelves.ts`):

- **The shelves.** A hall is 8 boards, the first 0.2 m from the floor and one every 0.28 m, in the wall of 5 m by 1.2 m by 2.4 m of section 3.1. A card is laid out for the number of cards the hall holds in the whole run (the cards of its record in the plan), so it never moves when another arrives: its place is its rank by (`shown_s`, then sample) among them, numbered along the top board from the west end and then down. A row holds the cards divided by 8, rounded up; a card is 0.8 of its pitch wide (at most 0.053 m) and 3.4 times as tall (at most 0.18 m, so the top row stays under the wall and the pennants have 0.06 m above a card). The default run has 391, 394 and 394 cards in A, B and C: 49 to 50 in a row, a pitch of 0.092 m and a card 0.053 m by 0.18 m.
- **The card.** From the top, four squares: the unit, `sampled_at`, the context and the value; a stored slot is a solid square and a slot not stored a hollow square drawn with a dashed line (shape, not colour alone). A green bar at the foot of a card that is knowable (from `available_s`; A and B store no availability, so none of their cards ever shows it). A pennant, a triangle on the top edge, for each rule of the record's own findings raised by the clock instant; a sheet behind the card, peeking out to the upper right, for each version written by then beyond the one in front (a stack exists only where `append_only_versions` keeps versions: where the record overwrites in place the plan holds one version). At most four pennants and four sheets are drawn. Each card is one instance of a quad with a small shader that is given four numbers (`lookOf` of `card_look.ts`: a bit for each slot stored, knowable, the sheets, the pennants); the whole of the halls is one draw call. The first build made the card of seven instances (the paper, the squares, the bar, the pennants, the sheets), and in the software renderer of the QA that took the frame from 17 ms to 50 ms (section 8). Cards at night keep half their brightness; the halls that are not the lens keep 0.55 of theirs.
- **When a card changes.** The instants the plan lacked are the record's own stamps (`plant_records.ts`): a result row is written at its `ingested_at` where the system stores it and otherwise at its `measured_at`; version k exists from the earliest such stamp of a row of version k or later (`version_s`); a finding carries no stamp, so a rule is read to have raised it when the row it targets was written (the stamp of its result, or `shown_s` for a target that is the sample or a source event). The shelves change only at those instants and at `shown_s` and `available_s`, so `hallsEpoch` (the number of them not later than the clock) tells a frame whether to draw them again.
- **The lens.** The chip in the HUD has a button for each architecture of the layout; the pressed one is the hall the record panel reads and the cards list offers, and the other halls are dimmed (their cards, and their signs). A hall whose record has not arrived is a disabled button that says so. A click on a card reads that card's own hall, and the lens goes there; moving the lens closes a record that is open in another hall, so the panel never reads a hall other than the lens.
- **The sign.** Each hall carries its architecture id over the shelves and, when the camera is within 16 m and the record has arrived, the flags it has on (ids, language-neutral; a dash for none); the lens hall wears a disc drawn by the style sheet. The sign is DOM text and is the one part of the scene that Chromium rasterises a little differently from one run to the next, so the pixel comparison of the QA leaves its layer out (section 10).
- **Picking.** A click that reaches the shelves is turned into a card by arithmetic on the front face of each hall (`placeAt`: the column of the pitch it falls in and the board it is over, then the card at that place if it is shown), with no ray against thousands of instances, and the nearest hall wins; a click that meets no card walks the avatar. The keyboard path is the cards list (the newest twelve cards of the lens hall at the clock instant, one button each: `Tab` reaches it, `Enter` opens the record, `Esc` closes it and the focus returns to the button); the clock reaches any other.

*Absence is truth, so a hall never shows it.* A row the record does not hold (a duplicate dropped by `ingest_idempotency`, a draw skipped by the volume budget, a lost rerun, a field of a version that was overwritten) leaves no mark in the hall, because a record cannot know what it lacks. With the truth overlay on, a dashed placeholder appears at the slot where the plant produced a row and names the architecture that holds none. This is the comparison the lab exists to make, drawn at the place it happens.

*As built in P5b* (`engine/src/experiments/assembly/lab/plant_truth.ts`, `src/plant/placeholders.ts`, `gl/overlay.ts`, the switch in `Hud.tsx`, `RecordPanel.tsx`):

- **The label.** Every overlay element carries "Simulation's own view, not available to any record" as text in the element: the switch, the label of each rail, the truth block of the record panel. The elements are marked `data-overlay` and the end-to-end check fails on one without the sentence.
- **What the overlay draws, and what waits.** Of the four absences listed above P5b draws two, both as the absence of a card: a draw skipped by the volume budget (in every hall, since no record can hold a row of a draw that left no sample) and a drawn sample the hall holds no card for (the run drew it and this architecture holds no run and no result row of it; in the default run A holds none for U06-D09-1, U13-D03-2 and U18-D03-1, where a label error keyed the rows to another sample, and B and C hold a card for every drawn sample). The record panel with the overlay on already names the lost rerun and the duplicate replay among the errors the injector materialised on the sample.
- **Where a placeholder stands.** The shelves are laid out for the cards the hall holds, a card never moves, and a row that does not exist has no place among them, so the placeholders stand on a rail on top of the hall's wall: a row of tags 0.25 m tall on the wall's upper edge (2.4 m), in order of the instant the plant produced the row (the draw, `drawn_s` of the plan's sample), then by sample. The rail is laid out for the placeholders the hall has in the whole run, as the shelves are, so a tag never moves when another appears; a tag appears at its instant. A tag is a dashed hollow outline of a card (no fill; a slash through it for a skipped draw), its width 0.8 of its pitch (at most 0.14 m, the pitch at most 0.18 m), and the hall's rail wears a label in the DOM: the architecture that holds none, how many, and the sentence of the label above. The halls that are not the lens keep 0.55 of their brightness, as their cards do.
- **The record panel.** With the overlay on, the panel shows beside the record the truth column (the lineage screen's `LineageTruth`), the three questions of the lens architecture with their verdicts (`RecordSvg` draws them under the record when it is given the answers and the truth, as the lineage screen does) and the errors the injector materialised on the sample in that architecture (`RealisedErrors`, taken out of `LineageColumns`); the words are the lineage screen's. ## 4. From event log to motion

### 4.1 What the page receives: the scene plan

The Worker runs the engine and the recorders for one seed, then builds a `ScenePlan`: plain data, a tree of numbers, strings, booleans, null and arrays, listing only what the scene draws. It is a projection of the truth run with the hidden fields of `engine/src/observe.ts` removed (`HIDDEN_FIELDS`) and the truth-only types dropped (`TRUTH_ONLY_TYPES`), plus the cards of the recorded datasets; it is not a log and carries no sensor tick.

```ts
interface ScenePlan {
  schema_version: "1"; seed: number; horizon_s: number;          // integer seconds from the scenario start; day 0 is a Monday (spec 2.1)
  layout: LayoutSpec;                                            // the input of layoutOf (section 3.1)
  units: { id: string; bay: number; slot: number; profile: string; batch: string | null; start_s: number | null; end_s: number | null }[];
  analyzers: { id: string; profile: string; group: string }[];
  staff: { id: string; on_duty: [number, number][] }[];          // the roster as shift intervals (A098, A201)
  samples: { id: string; unit: number; operator: number | null; planned_s: number; drawn_s: number | null; arrived_s: number | null; label: string | null;
             kind: "routine" | "confirm"; skipped: boolean;
             visits: { analyzer: number; queued_s: number; started_s: number; run_started_s: number; ended_s: number; attempt: number; qc: string | null; setup_s: number; diluted: boolean }[] }[];
  marks: { t: number; unit: number; kind: string; source: "rule" | "scheduled" | "scripted" | null; item: string | null }[];   // feed, temperature_shift, alarm, harvest, unit_stopped, medium_addition, equipment_use, signal_observed, deviation_opened
  windows: { analyzer: number; kind: "qc" | "maintenance" | "closed"; from_s: number; to_s: number }[];
  calibrations: { analyzer: number; t: number }[];               // one entry for each (analyzer, instant) of calibration_recorded, by instant then analyzer (added in P4)
  records: { architecture: string; flags_on: string[]; cards: { sample: number; shown_s: number; available_s: number | null;
            slots: { name: "unit" | "sampled_at" | "context" | "value"; stored: boolean; basis?: "sampled_at" | "measured_at" }[];
            findings: { rule: string; raised_s: number }[]; version_s: number[] }[] }[];   // P5a: the instants of a version and of a finding replace `versions` and the bare rule ids
}
```

`records` is the halls built so far: `plant_ready` holds the hall of the first architecture of the spec and each `plant_halls` message one more (section 5.5), while `layout.architectures` names every hall from the first answer. The spike's plan for the default scenario (394 drawn samples, 192 feeds, three records, a slimmer card than above) was 28,112 bytes of JSON and posted in 0.3 ms; with the three halls and their cards of P5a the messages of the default scenario are 457,378 bytes in all (1,569,829 bytes at N = 100). Rules: ids are strings (a sample is `U10-D08-1`), indices point into the arrays of the same plan, times are integer seconds of the run, and no field holds a value of a result, a true state or a noise term (a result value appears only as the `value` slot's stored flag; the number itself is read in the record panel through `plant_record`).

### 4.2 The time base: a stateless scene

- The run's time is the engine's integer seconds. The view clock `t_view` is a real number. Wall time advances it by `dt x speed` only while playing; nothing else reads wall time and no result contains a wall-clock value.
- `sceneAt(plan, t)` is a pure function that returns the state of every drawn object at `t` (positions, phases, lamp states, cards shown). It depends on nothing but its arguments, so seeking, pausing and playing backwards need no replay, and a screenshot at a fixed `t` shows the same scene every time. A property test calls it for a shuffled list of instants and compares each answer with the one from an ordered list.
- Playback: play and pause (`Space`); speed presets of 30, 120 (the default), 600 and 3600 simulated seconds per wall second; step to the next or previous event of the picked object or kind; seek by a scrubber over the horizon with ticks for day boundaries, actions and alarms, and by keys (plus or minus one hour, plus or minus one day). A frame never skips the start of a carry that the reader could have seen: at the top speed one frame advances well under a sample's transport time (10 to 25 min, A018).
- Lights follow the roster: the floor is lit inside the staffed window of the day (A098, A201) and dim outside; the analyzers wear a "closed" sign in their closed windows (the engine's `closed` windows) and the queue builds in front of a sign for the reader to see.

*As built in P4* (`src/plant/scene_at.ts` with `scene_look.ts` and `scene_index.ts`, `clock.ts`, `playback.ts`, `events.ts`; the metres and the simulated seconds that the log does not give are one table, `LOOK`, and are view constants, not domain numbers, so no ledger id belongs to them):

- **What the log gives and what the view invents.** Every instant the log has is drawn at that instant: the draw, the arrival at the lane, the start, the run start and the end of a visit, a mark, a window edge, a calibration. A walk to a vessel has no event, so the figure leaves early enough to arrive at the draw instant at a pace of `LOOK.walk_mps` simulated metres for each simulated second; the stays, the gliding of a tube between the lane and the analyzer and the stretches in which a pulse, a beacon, a tag, a slip, a card or a lamp stays lit are view constants too. A figure is continuous: when two draws of one operator are too close for the pace, it walks faster rather than jump (the engine does give one operator two draws within seconds). The one exception is two draws of one operator at the same second at two vessels, where the later one wins at that second.
- **The queue.** The tubes waiting in the lane of a group are those with `queued_s <= t < started_s`, in the order of their `started_s` (ties by `queued_s`, then sample, then attempt), so the front slot always holds the next tube to enter. Slots fan out from the middle of the tray (the front, then right, left, right ...) in rows toward the aisle. A tube that arrives rides to the slot it will hold at its arrival; later ranks change by whole slots when a tube ahead enters an analyzer (no animation of the shift).
- **Staff.** A rostered operator is drawn while on duty. They walk from a home station on the aisle in front of the bench to the vessel of a draw, stay there for the draw (carrying the tube in hand), and walk home, or straight to the next vessel when the gap does not allow the trip home. The skipped draw has no operator in the log (the event names none), so no figure walks to it: the vessel shows the volume-cap tag.
- **Tubes.** One tube from the draw on: in the hand at the vessel, on a carrier along the aisles to the lane (the true transport time of the log, A018), in a lane slot, gliding into the analyzer at `visit_started`, in the analyzer (setup, then run) until `visit_finished`, then to the rack and gone; a rerun returns it to the lane with a ring, or, when its next run starts at the second the last one ends, glides it straight to the other analyzer (found against the real plan: the first draft put it back in the lane for that second and the tube jumped). The cue per phase is a shape above the tube, never colour alone (an arrowhead while it travels, a disc in the lane, a ring in the analyzer, a cube when it is done); the label glyph is a shape by method; a diluted visit has a band on the tube.
- **Lights.** `daylight` is 1 inside the union of the shifts and falls to `LOOK.dim` over `LOOK.ramp_s` outside it: a function of `t`, so a screenshot at a fixed instant is the same every time.
- **Clock.** The clock is a plain state `{ t, speed, playing }` and pure functions in `clock.ts` (advance by wall seconds, seek, step); `playback.ts` holds it for the page and tells whoever subscribed. The frame loop of the scene is the only caller that passes wall seconds, and `advance` adds them only while playing; the clock stops at the horizon. The page starts playing when the scene is drawn, at the opening of the first staffed window, and starts paused under reduced motion (section 9). Keys: `Space` plays and pauses, `1` to `4` pick a preset, `,` and `.` step to the previous and the next event, `[` and `]` seek an hour back and on, `{` and `}` a day. The keys leave a text field, a select and a text area alone; they work with the focus on a button or on the scrubber, except that Space is the button's own key. The next event is the next instant of the table of section 4.3 that the plan carries, over the whole floor with nothing picked, and, with an object picked (section 3.4), the next instant of that object's own events: a sample's draw, arrival and the instants of its visits; a reactor's inoculation, marks and the draws on it; an analyzer's visits, windows and calibrations; an operator's draws (`objectTimes` of `events.ts`, from the plan alone); a step pauses the clock. Without WebGL there is no scene to give the clock its wall seconds, so the clock stays at the plan's start and the text alternative describes that instant; the time bar is part of the scene's view. The scrubber is a native range input over the horizon in whole minutes, with a tick for every day boundary and the clock reading as its spoken value.
- **The halls.** `hallsAt(plan, t)` (`src/plant/halls_at.ts`) is `sceneAt`'s partner for the record halls: a pure function of the plan and the instant, with no state between calls, returning for each hall of `plan.records` the cards present at `t` (a card from its `shown_s`, in the order of its place), each with its slots, whether it is knowable, its pennants and the depth of its version stack, and the counts of cards shown and knowable. The seek property holds as for `sceneAt` (a shuffled list of instants gives the answers of an ordered one). The present cards are a prefix of the order of the places, found by a binary search.
- **The text side.** The tables of the text alternative follow the clock at most once a second of wall time while playing and at once when the clock is seeked or paused; its live region says the newest event of the last two seconds of wall time (one line, every two seconds at most), in both languages, and can be switched off.

## 5. Operator actions

### 5.1 Principle

An operator action is plain data that says what the reader did and when, in the units of the run. The list of actions is part of the plant's spec; the page sends it, the Worker checks it, applies it to the scenario, recomputes the run from its start and answers a new scene plan; the view resumes at the instant of the action. The page computes nothing: no result, no consequence. `at` is the clock reading when the reader acted, in integer seconds and a whole minute (scheduled events sit at whole minutes, A200); the page rounds it up to the next whole minute.

*As built in P6a* (`src/plant/{BottomBar,ActionForm,ActionList}.tsx`, `act_form.ts`, `action_list.ts`, `refusal.ts`, `board.ts`):

- **The bar.** The five core actions are buttons with an icon and a label; the three optional ones (`raise_alarm`, `change_lot`, `set_labelling`) are in an overflow, a button "More actions" that opens a short list under the bar. Every button is reachable by the keyboard. A button opens one small form above the bar (one form at a time; `Esc` or its button closes it and the focus goes back to the button that opened it). A form holds the clock paused while it is open, so that the instant it prints is the instant it sends; closed without sending, it gives the clock back as it was (playing, if it was playing). The bar is disabled while a recompute is in flight (section 5.5).
- **Targets are the ids the plan and `ready` have**: the units of `plan.units`, the analyzers of `plan.analyzers`, the halls of `plan.layout.architectures`, the flags of `ready.flags`, the two capture paths, the three labelling methods and the rows of the sampling boards. A form is prefilled from the picked object where there is one (a reactor for a feed or an alarm, an analyzer for a window or a lot change) and a flag form starts on the opposite of the flag's state in the hall as the plan has it. A choice outside these ids cannot be made in a form; a link can carry one and the Worker refuses it.
- **`at`.** The clock reading rounded up to the next whole minute (`atOf` of `act_form.ts`: 28,800 s stays 28,800 s, 28,800.4 s and 28,859 s become 28,860 s). The other instants (`to`, `from`, `effective`) are typed as a day of the run and a time of day (the move form fixes the day); each is printed as a clock reading with its ledger entry A228 as assumed, as Setup prints an assumed input (`<Ref>`), and so is every instant in the reader's list. The ids of the thing an action perturbs (the column "Ids the panel prints") are printed under its form the same way; the dictionary names them, so the ledger view of the page holds them (`scripts/lab_ledger.py --write` added four entries to the full and the public view: A228, A036, A061 and A202; A036 and A061 follow to sources below main-reread, so their chips say provisional, as the chips of Setup do). One id is left out: A197 of the alarm follows to sources of the Break-out screen that are not re-read, and the public view must not name it (`src/ledger.test.ts`), so the form of the alarm prints the entry of its instant only; it comes with the re-read.
- **The page checks nothing the Worker checks.** A form is complete when every field has a value, and that is all the page asks; whether a value is allowed is the Worker's (section 5.6).
- *As built in P6b:* the hall of a flag form is chosen by the id the plan shows and sent as the record the run began with (section 3.2, "As built in P6b"); the form also opens from a cell of the flags board with the hall, the flag and the opposite state filled in.

### 5.3 Two classes of action

- **Plant actions** change the truth run. They act on the future of `at` only: nothing the reader sees before `at` changes (the causality rule; the spike measured it: the events before `at` were identical for a feed and for a moved draw, section 8). They enter the scenario as scripted events or as the scenario fields of the table.
- **Lab settings** change what is recorded and not what happened: the truth run's log hash is unchanged (a test). An architecture is a property of the lab, not of an instant, so the record is re-made for the whole run and earlier cards change too; the HUD says so in one line ("applies to the whole run"), and the view still resumes at `at`. The author chose this reading: a flag switched during a run applies to the whole run. The alternative, a flag with an effective-from instant, would be a recorder that changes behaviour mid-run, a mechanism the spec does not define. A flag switched in a record hall makes a derived architecture document: the named record's document with the flag overridden, its id the base id plus `_minus_<flag>` for each flag it turns off and `_plus_<flag>` for each flag it turns on, in the order of the flag table (one flag off in C gives the existing `c_context_minus_<flag>`); two switches of one flag keep the one with the later `at`.
- **The one exception, `set_labelling`.** The labelling method is a lab setting (the recorders and the injector read it, A047, A061), but `label_method` also decides the instant and the method of the `label_bound` events of the truth log (spec 2.2), so the strict truth hash changes and no other event does. Its checks (section 5.5) therefore compare the logs without the `label_bound` events; the strict hash still goes to `plant_equal`. Found in P2: the first draft of this section listed it among the settings that leave the truth hash unchanged.

### 5.4 Keys, order and determinism

- Targets are named by id (unit, sample, analyzer, lot, equipment item), never by position in a list, so an added action moves no other draw (spec section 3, rule 3).
- An action adds no random draw of its own. Where a mechanism needs one it takes the canonical tuple `(run_seed, <target id>, "-", 0, "operator:<kind>")`, listed in a table of the action keys and checked for collisions as the run's keys are. In this build none of the eight needs one: a scripted feed, alarm, maintenance window and lot change reuse the engine's own mechanisms, `move_draw` changes an instant and leaves the keyed draws of its sample as they are, and the three lab settings change what is read. The table (`ACTION_DRAWS` in `actions.ts`) is empty for that reason, and a test runs a scenario with every action and finds no key whose purpose starts with `operator:` and no key that two different purposes share.
- The Worker sorts the list by `(class, at, kind, target id, parameters)` and names the actions `act-1`, `act-2` ... in that order; the entry order never reaches the scenario, so the same set of actions gives the same scenario and the same bytes in any order of entry.
- No wall clock, no entry timestamp. A duplicate (same kind, target and instant) is refused. The list is capped at 16 actions per run (a view constant that bounds the link and the recompute, not a domain number).

### 5.5 Recompute and resume

1. The reader acts; the page adds the action to its list and posts `plant_run` with the whole list and a new epoch. The view holds the clock at `at` and shows "recomputing" in the HUD (a status line, no spinner under reduced motion).
2. The Worker checks the list (section 5.6), applies it to a copy of the scenario document, runs the scenario and the recorders from the start, builds the plan, and answers `plant_ready` with the epoch, `resume_at` (the first `at` of the actions that changed), the hashes of section 5.7 (the truth hash and the cells digest of each hall built so far) and two checks: `prefix_equal` (the events before `at` equal those of the previous answer, expected true for a plant action) and `truth_equal` (the truth hash equals the previous one, expected true for a lab setting). A newer epoch cancels an unfinished older one; the page drops an answer whose epoch is not the latest.
3. The page replaces its plan, sets the clock to `resume_at`, and draws `sceneAt(plan, resume_at)`. A false check is shown as "causality check failed", keeps the old plan and is a defect to report, never a state to tolerate.
4. Undo removes an action from the list and recomputes the same way.

There is no incremental update: the engine has no checkpoint and a recompute from the start of the default scenario costs a fraction of a second (section 8). For a larger fleet the records dominate the cost (section 8, Node times), so the Worker answers `plant_ready` as soon as the truth run and the lens record are built and sends the other halls afterwards, one `plant_halls` message each, which the page draws as they arrive; the floor and the lens hall never wait for the others. The default scenario needs none of this, but the Worker does it for every run. The Worker keeps the last run keyed by the canonical JSON of its spec, so a drill-down of the same run (a lineage view of a sample the plant shows) does not recompute; the cache must not change a byte, and the equality tests of section 5.7 cover it.

*As built in P5a* (`engine/src/experiments/assembly/lab/plant_stage.ts` and `plant_record.ts`, `src/worker/worker.ts`, `src/plant/plant_state.ts`):

- **The two builds.** `runPlant` builds every architecture in one call of the recorders and stays the reference. `stagePlant` makes the truth run once, then runs the recorders for one architecture at a time on that run (`runSeed` with that one architecture): the first at once, so that `plant_ready` carries the plan with that hall, its cell digest and the truth hash, and a function for each of the others. The Worker posts `plant_ready`, then for each other architecture, in the order of the spec, waits one turn of the event loop, checks that its epoch is still the newest, builds the hall, checks again and posts one `plant_halls` (`records` of one hall and `cells` of one digest). A newer epoch seen between two halls stops the ones still pending and nothing is posted for them; it is not an error, since the plan of the older epoch was answered. A test proves the plan assembled from `plant_ready` and its `plant_halls` byte-equal (canonical JSON) to the plan of `runPlant`, with the same truth hash and the same digests, for the default scenario in the order A, B, C and for another order with a feed.
- **What the Worker keeps.** `PlantKept` gains the halls built so far (the recorded dataset of each, and the ids of the samples it holds a card of), filled as they are built; `plant_record` reads them (section 6). Nothing of it leaves the Worker but the rows of one card.
- **What the page does.** `plant_state.ts` keeps the plan of `plant_ready` as it is (the clock and the scene are made from that object and are not rebuilt) and merges each `plant_halls` beside it, in the order of the layout, with its digest; a hall of an epoch that is not the newest asked for or not the plan on screen is dropped, and so is one already merged. The scene, the HUD, the text alternative and the lens chip read the plan with the halls that have arrived; the objects list has a hall for every architecture of the layout from the start, so it agrees with the floor.
- **Times** (section 8): at the default size `plant_ready` takes about two thirds of the time of one build and the last hall a little under it; at N = 100 the floor and the first hall are there in about half the time.

*As built in P6a* (`plant_state.ts`, `useOpening.ts`, `ActionList.tsx`, `useAddress.ts`, `usePlant.ts`):

- **One request at a time.** The bar and the undo buttons are disabled while an action request is in flight, so a refusal always belongs to the action just sent and an action can never be kept by a request that was cancelled. The store keeps the spec the page asked for (its list is the reader's list) and, while the request is in flight, the spec before it.
- **Accepted.** The page posts `plant_run` with the whole list and a new epoch; the clock is held at `at` and the HUD says that the run is recomputing (a line in a live region, with a turning ring that is not drawn under reduced motion). On `plant_ready` of that epoch the page replaces its plan and seeks the clock to `resume_at`, and plays on if the clock was playing when the reader acted; the instant of a link or of the reader's last leaving does not apply to it.
- **Refused.** The spec goes back to the one before, the plan on screen, its clock and the list are as they were, and the refusal is shown at the form (section 5.6). A refused action is not kept in the list.
- **A false check.** A `plant_ready` of that epoch whose `prefix_equal` or `truth_equal` is false is not drawn: the plan on screen and the list stay, and the form says "causality check failed" with the name of the check. It is a defect to report, never a state to tolerate (step 3 above).
- **The list and the undo.** The HUD lists the actions of the spec, each in words with its instant, in the canonical order of section 5.4: a copy of the Worker's order in `action_list.ts`, kept the same by a test against `canonicalActions`. A lab setting is marked "whole run". Each has an undo, which removes that action from the list and posts the rest the same way (the view resumes at the undone action's instant, which is the `resume_at` the Worker answers).
- **Found in P6a: the prefix check and the queue's number.** The first run of `plant_prefix` on the default scenario answered `prefix_equal` false for `analyzer_down`: the one difference before `at` was the `seq` of a `request_queued` of a request queued again (a rerun), 549,270 s into the run, by two. `seq` of that event is the tie-break number of the analyzer's event queue, which starts above the numbers of the requests and which the start and the end of each window take two of before the run, so one more window moves the number of every rerun, earlier in the run too; no time, type or id changes. The small default scenario of the P2 proof has no rerun before day 3, which is why it did not show. `compare` of `plant.ts` leaves `seq` of a `request_queued` out of the comparison (`engine/tests/plant/prefix_seq.test.ts`: red on the default scenario, green after; it also pins the shift of two and that a different seed is still a false check). Nothing else changes: the strict hash still has the number in it.
- **The scene is rebuilt, the renderer is not.** A new plan makes a new scene (the clock, the layout and the objects are made from it), but the renderer and its canvas are kept from one scene to the next (`gl/stage.ts`), and the materials of the scene before are not disposed, because disposing the last material that uses a program deletes the program. The first build of the bar remade the renderer with each plan and the page then took 1.2 to 1.5 s from `plant_ready` to the first frame in the software renderer, so a round trip was 1.4 to 1.7 s against a Worker answer of 0.2 s; with the renderer kept it is 0.3 s (section 8). Leaving the plant frees the renderer and its context (`releaseStage`).
- **The address.** Once a run has settled, and again after every render of the route (the navigation link of the plant, Home and Back can change the address under it), the page writes the plant link of the spec into the address with `history.replaceState` (never a new history entry, so Back leaves the plant and does not step through its actions), when the list is not empty or the address already carries `actions` and the address does not hold the link already; the link has no `t`. A reload or a copied address reopens the same run with its actions (`plantQuery` and `plantFromQuery` of P5c, with a test for three actions in any order of entry).

### 5.6 What is refused, and why

A refusal is an `error` with `code: "refused"` and a reason (section 6); the bar shows the action disabled with the reason, so the reader learns the rule at the place they met it.

| Refused | Reason | Code |
|---|---|---|
| A target instant earlier than `at`, or a target already planned, started or over at `at` (a sample already planned, a window already begun) | The past is not rewritten; the view resumes at `at` and everything before it must be unchanged | `past` |
| A unit, sample, analyzer, flag, lot or item that the run does not have; a kind that is not in the table | A request is user input (a link); ids are checked as for `RunSpec` | `unknown_target` |
| A draw moved outside the staffed window or to another day; a feed or an alarm outside the culture of its unit | The staffed window and the draw rule (A201, A202), or the culture of the unit (after its inoculation, before its harvest), checked by the Worker before the engine runs; any other value the engine itself rejects arrives as `engine_error` with the engine's message, verbatim | `outside_window` |
| Writing a value: a result, a recorded field, a true state, a probe offset, a noise term | No instrument produced it; the page never computes a result and a record cannot be edited by its reader. There is no such kind, and the UI never offers one | `unknown_target` |
| Anything whose answer would show a hidden field: "test this sample for contamination now", "what is the true value"; placing a contamination (`place_contamination` is not offered) | Truth is shown only by the truth overlay, on request, under its label. Confirming tests exist only as the scenario's own scripted response with its turnaround (A176), visible as events | `unknown_target` |
| Acting on a recorded result: hold, release, reject, readjust or stop a unit because a record said so (the kinds `hold`, `release`, `reject`, `readjust`, `stop_unit`) | That closes the loop; the plant is open loop (ADR-0004: detection never changes the truth run). The closed loop exists only in SC8 and in the challenge's decision variant | `closed_loop` |
| Planting an error: a label error, a re-key, a replay | The challenge plants by keyed draws and relies on the reader not knowing; a free plant would also hand the reader the reveal of `outcomeOf`. Parked (section 12, item 9) | `unknown_target` |
| Any action or truth request while a challenge is open on the same run | The same lock as `lineage` (`lab_ui.md` section 4, rule 2): the same run is the resolved run, so a plant spec that writes a setting out at its default value or repeats the scenario's own profiles is the run of the challenge (audit A1, F7) | `locked` |
| The seventeenth action; a duplicate; a second move of a sample that another action already moves; an analyzer window that overlaps another window of the same analyzer | The cap and the duplicate rule of section 5.4; one move per sample (a second one would have to say which instant it replaces: undo the first); one window per analyzer at a time (section 5.2) | `limit` |
| A setting of the plant's spec that no plant can have (a count that is not a whole number of one or more, a probability outside 0 to 1, a viability not above 0 or above 100, a concentration below 0, a duration not above 0, or any value its kind of `lab_ui.md` section 4 does not allow, a negative rate for one), an integration step or a sensor cadence that does not divide one minute, a setting that places a scheduled event off a whole minute, or a run above the size cap | The settings of a spec are checked in one place for every request that embeds a `RunSpec` (`lab_ui.md` section 4, "The settings of a spec"; audit A1, F5 and F6); the plant's link is user input like any other | `invalid_spec` |
| A value outside the ledger range of its id | Not refused: a warning chip beside the input, as on Setup (`lab_ui.md` section 3), and the run goes on | none |
| Changing the seed or the scenario | Not an action: it starts a new run (Setup) | none |

*As built in P6a* (`refusal.ts`, `src/i18n/{en,ko}_act.ts`): a refusal is shown at the form that sent the action, as a block that is announced when it appears (`role="alert"`), with one sentence for the reason in the page's language (`past`, `unknown_target`, `outside_window`, `closed_loop`, `limit`, and `locked` for a challenge open on the run), a seventh for any other error of the Worker (an `engine_error` or an `invalid_spec`), and the Worker's message beside it in a `lang="en"` element, with the place of the action in the list as entered. The sentence is a word about the rule and states no number; the bounds are in the message. A refusal that comes with a link (an action the address carries) is the plant's problem as before and is shown at the head of the route with the same sentence for its reason. The default run shows `past` (a move of a late sample, a time before `at`), `outside_window` (a feed on a reactor not yet in culture, a draw moved outside the staffed day) and `limit` (the same feed twice, a second move of one sample) through the bar, `locked` with a challenge open on its run, and `unknown_target` and `closed_loop` through a link.

The unit test of the action kinds compares the kinds of the code with the first column of the table of section 5.2, read from this file (the pattern of `tests/test_explainer_flags.py`), so a kind cannot be added without a row here.

### 5.7 How a recomputed run is proven equal to the run with the actions written in by hand

Let S0 be the base scenario document, `a` an action, `applyActions(S0, [a])` the pure function that returns the scenario with the action applied, and H the same scenario written by hand as a fixture under `tests/fixtures/plant/` (typed from the mapping of section 5.2, never produced by the code under test; a fixture holds the actions and the fields of the scenario that must result, which replace those fields of S0). The hashes are those of `lab_ui.md` section 7: the strict log hash of the truth run (sensor ticks excluded) and, for each architecture, the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of its metric cells (`cells`).

1. **Mapping.** `applyActions(S0, [a])` equals H as JSON (canonical key order). A wrong field, a wrong unit or a minute off fails here.
2. **Run.** The truth hash and the cells digest of the run of `applyActions(S0, [a])` equal those of the run of H, for each action kind and for two or three actions together.
3. **Worker path.** The `plant_ready` of the Worker for `(spec, [a])` carries the same hashes. A command-line reference, built like `lab_reference_cli.ts` under the engine's flags from the hand fixtures, writes `plant_reference.json`; because a seed gives the same bytes in Node and in every browser (ADR-0019), the page in Chromium must show hashes equal to the Node run of H. The end-to-end check `plant_equal` compares them.
4. **Order of entry.** Every permutation of a set of three actions gives one scenario and one set of hashes.
5. **Undo.** Removing the action returns the base hashes. The spike returned to the base hash every time.
6. **Repeat.** The same list asked ten times gives one hash. The spike gave one hash in ten of ten.
7. **Prefix.** For a plant action, the events before `at` equal those of the previous run, the same list without the action (`prefix_equal`, section 5.5). The Worker computes it by comparing the two runs event by event; the QA reads it from the answer.
8. **Lab settings.** The truth hash equals the base hash and only the cells of the architecture that changed differ.
9. **Mutations**, each run in a scratch copy and reverted, must make a test fail: the action applied to the neighbouring unit, `at` rounded to the wrong minute, an action dropped from the list, a hand fixture a minute off, a derived architecture whose flag is not overridden.

### 5.8 Engine work an action needs

Everything but `move_draw` maps onto fields the scenario already has, and a derived architecture document (section 5.3) needs no engine change because the recorders take documents. `move_draw` is a new scripted event type, built in P2: a clause in `docs/design/simulation_spec.md` section 2.2, an additive change of `schemas/scenario.schema.json` (`scripted_events[]` gains a second shape, `{ type: "move_draw", sample_id, at, to }`; every earlier document still validates), and one change in `engine/src/sampling.ts` that replaces the planned instant before the keyed delay is added. The regression rule of spec section 11.1 holds: with no `move_draw` the golden event logs and every pinned hash stay byte-identical. The spike had emulated it with one extra line in a copy of `sampling.ts`; that line is the whole behaviour. The orchestrator ruled (section 12, item 10) that it needs no ADR of its own: it is a scenario addition under ADR-0021.

## 6. The Worker protocol additions

These add to section 4 of `lab_ui.md`; nothing there changes except the amendment of rule 1 below.

```ts
interface PlantSpec { run: RunSpec; seed: number; actions: Action[] }     // run.architectures is the set of record halls

type PlantRequest =
  | { type: "plant_run"; id: number; epoch: number; spec: PlantSpec }
  | { type: "plant_truth"; id: number; epoch: number; pick: { sample_id: string } | { halls: true } | { unit_id: string } | { breakout: true } }   // `unit_id` and `breakout` wait (rule 8)
  | { type: "plant_record"; id: number; epoch: number; architecture: string; sample_id: string };   // the drill-down of lab_ui section 4 (`sample_record`), tied to an epoch

type PlantResponse =
  | { type: "plant_ready"; id: number; epoch: number; plan: ScenePlan; resume_at: number;
      hashes: { truth: string; cells: Record<string, string> }; checks: { prefix_equal: boolean | null; truth_equal: boolean | null };
      key: string }   // the key of the truth of the run without its actions (audit A1, F7; lab_ui.md section 4, rule 2)
  | { type: "plant_truth_view"; id: number; epoch: number; view: TruthOverlay }
  | { type: "plant_halls"; id: number; epoch: number; records: ScenePlan["records"]; cells: Record<string, string> }     // the halls that were not ready with plant_ready (section 5.5); the lens hall is in the plan
  | { type: "plant_record_view"; id: number; epoch: number; sample: SampleRecord };
// `error` gains code "refused" with a `reason` of section 5.6 and the index of the action

// The answer of `plant_truth`: the lineage view's own truth and nothing new (section 3.3). Fields are those of `LineageView` and `LineageColumn` (`lab_ui.md` section 8), as `lineageView` gives them.
type TruthOverlay =
  | { kind: "sample";                                                    // the pick { sample_id }
      sample: LineageView["sample"];                                     // picked_by is "reader"
      truth: LineageView["truth"];                                       // the unit, the true sampled_at, the true process context at the draw
      columns: { architecture: string; answers: LineageColumn["answers"]; realised_errors: LineageColumn["realised_errors"] }[] }   // one per hall built when asked, in the order of the spec; the rows of the record are not repeated (plant_record has them)
  | { kind: "halls";                                                     // the pick { halls: true }
      halls: { architecture: string; placeholders: { sample: number; why: "skipped" | "not_recorded" }[] }[] };   // sample: an index into the plan's `samples`; one entry per hall built when asked
```

Rules, each with a test:

1. **Amendment of rule 1 of `lab_ui.md` section 4.** The page still imports no engine module and cannot ask for a truth log, an event stream, the answer key or the raw quirk key. The plant adds one message whose payload is a scene plan: events projected onto drawn things, at their true instants, with every hidden field removed and every truth-only type dropped. The test walks the plan and fails on a key of `HIDDEN_FIELDS`, on a type of `TRUTH_ONLY_TYPES`, on a key outside an allow-list, and on a record card that holds a slot its architecture does not store. The orchestrator ruled it within ADR-0021 (section 12, item 6) and the amendment is written into rule 1 of `lab_ui.md` section 4.
2. The truth overlay is a separate request, answered only on the reader's switch, and refused with `locked` while a challenge is open on the same run; no `plant_ready` carries a verdict, a materialised error or a hidden onset.
3. Epochs: the Worker echoes the epoch on every message about a plant request, `error` included; a newer `plant_run` cancels an unfinished one; the page drops a stale answer. As built in P3: the epoch is a whole number that must exceed the last one the Worker saw (else `invalid_spec`, with the epoch echoed); plant runs are answered one at a time in the order they arrive; a run waits one turn of the event loop before it builds, and again checks, when its answer is built, that its epoch is still the newest; a run that is not answers with `error` code `cancelled` (epoch echoed) and neither posts a `plant_ready` nor becomes what the next answer is compared with (`PlantKept` is set only by an answer that was posted). The page's reducer (`src/plant/plant_state.ts`) drops a `plant_ready` and an `error` whose epoch is not the newest it asked for, and keeps the plan on screen until the newer answer arrives.
4. A request is checked, because a link is user input: kinds from the whitelist, ids against the run, bounds, count and duplicates (section 5.6); `invalid_spec` for a malformed request, `refused` for a well-formed action that a rule refuses.
5. The one-seed drill-down requests `lineage` and `breakout` accept `actions`, checked the same way, so a lineage view of a sample shows the run the plant shows (rule 9); `challenge_*` does not (it holds a blind plant). Results takes only the actions that name a unit or an analyzer (section 7), as an optional `actions` of the `run` request beside its `spec` (rule 10; `RunSpec` itself is unchanged).
6. Code zones: `three` is imported only under `src/plant/gl/**`; `layout.ts`, `scene_at.ts` and the controls' state import none, so Vitest runs them without WebGL; `src/worker/**` never imports `three` or the DOM; the page imports no engine module (the zone test of `lab_ui.md` section 2 gains `src/plant/**`, which is part of the page, and a rule of its own for `three`, in `eslint.config.js` and `engine/tests/contract/import_zones_src.test.ts`: types included, the Worker and the data module too). The Worker-facing functions live in `engine/src/experiments/assembly/lab/` as the others do (`plant.ts`, `actions.ts`, `plant_records.ts`).
7. **`plant_halls` and `plant_record` as built in P5a.** `plant_ready` carries the plan with the hall of the first architecture of the spec (`records`, and one digest in `hashes.cells`); each other architecture follows in a `plant_halls` of the same `id` and `epoch`. `plant_record` (`epoch`, `architecture`, `sample_id`) is answered at once with `plant_record_view`, the rows of the lineage view's record for that sample in that architecture (`sampleRecordOf`: nodes and edges, a null for a field the record does not store; no truth column, no answer, no verdict), read from the halls the Worker kept for that epoch with no recompute. An epoch other than the one the Worker holds (a newer plan was posted, or none) is `cancelled` with the epoch echoed, which the page drops; an architecture or a sample the run does not hold, a hall that is not built yet, or a sample the hall holds no card for is `refused` with the reason `unknown_target`, and no index (it is not an action): a record never answers for a row it does not hold, because an empty answer would show an absence, which only the truth overlay may show. A malformed request is `invalid_spec`. The page asks under the epoch of the plan on screen, remembers the id of its request, and keeps the answer of that request only; an error about it is the panel's problem and not the plant's.

8. **`plant_truth` as built in P5b** (`engine/src/experiments/assembly/lab/plant_truth.ts`). It reads what the Worker kept for the epoch (`PlantKept`: the checked spec, the truth log, the ids of the planned samples and, for each hall built, its dataset, the samples it holds a card of and the errors the injector materialised on it), with no recompute of the run: the injector is a keyed function of the run, called once for a hall when the hall is built (a few milliseconds), and the overlay of a sample is `lineageView` over the kept datasets, so it equals the lineage screen's view of the run the plant shows (a test compares them, deep, for every architecture of the spec). The order of the checks: a malformed request (no whole epoch, no pick, a pick of no kind) is `invalid_spec`; an epoch other than the one the Worker holds is `cancelled` with the epoch echoed (the page drops it); a challenge open on the run of the plan answers `locked`, checked at the moment of the request, so a challenge opened after the plan was posted locks the overlay of that plan (rule 2); a sample the run did not draw (planned and never drawn, skipped by the volume budget, or not in the run) is `refused` as `unknown_target`, with no index. The placeholders are asked for by a pick of their own, `{ halls: true }`, and are not part of the answer for a sample: they are the same for every sample and a hall holds few of them in the whole run (none for B and C of the default run), so one request for each epoch (and one more when a hall arrives after it) is smaller than a copy in each answer for a sample; a card or a tube asks for its sample only. `{ unit_id }` and `{ breakout: true }` are refused as `invalid_spec` ("not offered yet"): no lab function gives the truth of a unit (section 12, item 12) and the break-out truth waits for P7. The halls built when the request arrives are the ones answered; the page asks again when another hall arrives while the switch is on.
9. **`actions` on `lineage` and `breakout` as built in P5c** (`engine/src/experiments/assembly/lab/{lineage_request,lineage,breakout}.ts`, `src/worker/worker.ts`). Both requests take an optional `actions` (a list of the actions of section 5.2). Absent or an empty list, the answer is byte for byte what it was before (a test pins the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of the answer of the code before P5c, for the default scenario with seed 1 and for one break-out case, and the empty list takes the old path, not a second one that happens to agree). With actions, the order of the checks is that of `plant_run`: a malformed list is `invalid_spec`, a kind outside the table, a duplicate and the seventeenth action are `refused` (`unknown_target`, `closed_loop` or `limit`, with the index), then a run that is an unrevealed challenge is `locked` (the lock key is the run's scenario, settings, lists, capture path and seed; actions are not part of it, because a challenge holds a blind plant that no action reaches), then the bounds of section 5.6 against the run (`past`, `unknown_target`, `outside_window`, `limit`, with the index of the action as entered). The scenario with the actions applied is the one `plant_run` builds (`truthOf`: the same `applyActions`, the same `checkBounds`), so the lineage view of a sample through the drill-down is made from the run the plant shows, and its columns are the truth overlay's for that sample (a test compares them deep, for an actionless run and for one with a hand-written action). The `refused` error of these two requests carries `reason` and `index` as that of `plant_run` does, and no epoch (they are not plant requests). `breakout` applies the list to the scenario document of the case, the records being the architectures named by the request; `set_capture_path` is refused there as `unknown_target`, because the break-out records are made without the raw layer and the action would be accepted and do nothing, which a reader could take for an effect. `challenge_*` takes no `actions`: the Worker ignores no key silently, so a `challenge_start` that carries one is `invalid_spec`.
10. **`actions` on `run` as built in P6b** (`engine/src/experiments/assembly/lab/{actions,run}.ts`, `src/worker/worker.ts`). The `run` request of Results takes an optional `actions` (a field of the request, beside `spec`; the wording "in the `run` spec" of the earlier text meant this). The order of the checks is that of `plant_run` for the shape (`invalid_spec`), a kind, a duplicate and the seventeenth action (`refused` with reason and index), then a kind Results does not carry (`resultsActions`: `add_feed`, `raise_alarm`, `analyzer_down` and `change_lot`) is `refused` as `unknown_target` with the index, so that the Worker says what it will not carry and drops none silently, then the bounds of section 5.6 (`checkBounds`) against the run. The bounds of these four kinds do not depend on the seed (a unit's culture and an analyzer's windows are scenario settings; only the planned instant of a sample, which `move_draw` names and Results never carries, is a keyed draw), so they are checked once and a refusal names the index and no seed. Each seed's scenario is the scenario with the list applied by `applyActions`. A challenge's lock is not involved. The answer with none, or an empty list, is byte for byte what it was; the printed inputs of a run with actions list each instant with A228 (the window's length with A096) and the footer repeats the list. The design is `lab_ui.md` section 22.

## 8. Budgets, and the spike

**Budgets** (`prompts/00_master_build.md` section 4; a breach is a major finding). The first load of the lab stays at most 300 KB gzip (`gzip -c <file> | wc -c` over the files fetched on a cold load of `dist/lab/index.html`): the plant is a dynamically imported chunk and is not in it. The plant view's own chunk, its frame time and the action round trip get budgets of their own, set by the author from the spike (section 12, item 3).

*Why the plant chunk must stay out of the first load.* The public lab's first load at the S3 close is 135.2 KB (`docs/progress.md`, the S3 close). With the spike's crude scene the plant chunk alone is 136,113 B and the loader 2,053 B, so a first load that fetched them would be about 270 KB of 300 KB, with 30 KB of headroom for everything the scene still lacks. *The loader chunk as built.* The loader (`src/plant/loader.ts`: the check for a WebGL version two context on a scratch canvas, released at once, and the dynamic import) is a chunk of its own, named by one group in the build's `codeSplitting` (`src/vite.config.ts`), so that its 4 KB can be measured; Home and the plant route are in the entry chunk. **Measured in P6a** (the same machine, with a load average of 4 to 9 from other programs; Node v25.6.1 under the engine's flags, headless Chromium 156 with software WebGL at 1280 by 720): the clock playing at the default speed from day 8 at 08:30 with the cards drawn, the tubes moving and the truth overlay on, 300 frames after the first five: median 16.8 ms and 95th percentile 33.4 ms (the limits 50 and 80 ms), 51 draw calls a frame (49 in P5c: the plates and the posts of the sampling boards are two more instanced meshes). The action round trip, the default scenario with seed 1 and the halls A, B and C, five accepted actions one after another (a feed on U10 and one on U11, an alarm on U12, the capture path, a window of AN-1), timed in the page from the click on Apply to the first frame of the new plan: 314, 300, 308, 315, 297 ms, median 308 ms against the limits of 600 and 1,000 ms; of it the Worker's `plant_ready` takes 0.18 to 0.25 s and the page the rest (a new plan makes a new scene, but the renderer is kept from the scene before: with a new renderer for each plan the same five took 1,418, 1,729, 1,431, 1,700 and 1,621 ms, 1.2 to 1.5 s of them in the page, most of it the programs compiled again by the software renderer). The plant chunk is 142.8 KB gzip (`gzip -c`; limit 200 KB; 142.3 KB in P5c: the boards and the kept renderer), the loader chunk 1043 B, the first load of the local build 186.4 KB gzip over 6 files (176.9 KB in P5c: the bar, its forms, the list, the refusals and the board, and their texts in both languages). The Worker answers an action in the time it answers a run (an action is a run of the same scenario, section 5.5), 0.2 s at the default size.

A larger fleet, measured in Node under the engine's flags (the spike's set-up, one seed, the three records, medians of three warm runs): 24 reactors 0.26 s (truth 0.12 s, records 0.15 s), 50 reactors 0.77 s (0.22 s, 0.55 s), 100 reactors 3.0 s (0.39 s, 2.6 s), with 394, 839 and 1,376 drawn samples. The truth run grows about linearly and the records faster, so a round trip at N = 100 is several seconds with three halls; hence the progressive answer of section 5.5.

Other results of the spike that the design leans on: for a feed at day 8, 09:00 and for a draw moved 30 minutes at 07:30 the events before the action's instant were identical to the run without it (3,458 and 3,277 events); each action gave one hash in ten repeats and returned to the base hash on undo; with WebGL disabled in the browser (3D APIs off) the loader saw no WebGL2, showed the text alternative and did not request the plant chunk; the plan was 28,112 bytes. Not measured: a real GPU, Firefox and WebKit, a fleet larger than the default in the browser, a scene with authored models and textures, the cost of sound.

## 9. Fallbacks and access

- **No WebGL2.** The loader checks for a WebGL2 context before the dynamic import, so a reader without it never downloads the plant chunk (the spike confirmed this). The page then lands on Home and offers the text alternative below plus the S3 screens, which are the complete comparison without 3D. A 2D floor plan (the option 1 of ADR-0021) as a further fallback is a later batch if the author wants it (section 12, item 5).
- **Phone.** The overview camera is the default on a narrow viewport; tap to select, one finger to look, pinch to zoom; the bottom bar becomes a scrollable chip row; panels open as sheets. Two quality tiers: `high` (soft shadows, device pixel ratio up to 2, dust) and `low` (no shadows, ratio 1, no dust, fewer decor objects). `low` is the default under 600 px; on any device, if the median frame interval over the first 60 frames is above 50 ms the page offers `low` once and never switches silently; the choice is kept in `localStorage` inside `try/catch`.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion`): the plant starts paused; no camera easing (cuts), no auto-follow, no dust, no lamp pulses; the reader steps with "next event" and plays by choice, since the sample motion is the content. The click-to-walk moves the avatar without a glide. As built in P5b: a follow (section 3.4) under reduced motion does not glide along with the play; the camera cuts to the followed tube at each step and each seek of the paused clock (`followGoal` of `controls.ts`, tested by hand).
- **Keyboard and screen reader.** Every control is reachable: the order is the top bar, the scene, the objects list, the bottom bar. The objects list is a DOM list of every object of interest (reactors, analyzers, shelves, board) with the same panels and drill-downs, so the plant can be operated without aiming at a 3D object. The canvas has `role="img"`, a label and a description that points at the text summary.
- **Text alternative of the scene.** A static description of the layout; "the plant as a table" at the clock instant (reactors with their status, analyzers with their queue length and lamp, the record halls with their card counts); and an `aria-live="polite"` log of events ("08:31 sample U10-D08-1 drawn by OP-02 at reactor U10"), at most one line every two seconds of wall time and switchable off. All of it from the plan, in English and Korean. In P3 the instant is the plan's start, which is the opening of the first staffed window (day 0 begins at midnight and nobody is on the floor until the first shift opens: 08:00 of day 0 in the default scenario, with two operators on duty and the first reactor inoculated); the live region says that the plan is ready. As built in P4 the tables follow the clock (once a second of wall time while it plays, at once when it is paused or sought), the reactor status has the four words of the vessel lamp (idle, running, harvested, stopped), the analyzer lamp has setup and running besides the windows, and the live region carries the log: the newest event of the last two seconds of wall time, one line at most every two seconds, and a step to an event says that event, in both languages (twenty line texts in `src/i18n/*_plant.ts`, one for each kind of line of the log); a switch under it turns the reading off. As built in P5a the table of the halls has a column for the cards that are knowable besides the cards on the shelves (both from `hallsAt`), and a section after the objects list offers the newest cards of the lens hall as buttons with the legend of how to read a card. Without WebGL the clock stays at the plan's start.
- **English and Korean.** Every HUD, panel, action, refusal and live-region text is in the dictionaries (`src/i18n/*_plant.ts`) under the parity rules of `lab_ui.md` section 10: the same placeholders, the same ids, no digit in Korean that English lacks. Engine strings (a refusal's engine message) are shown verbatim in a `lang="en"` element under a translated heading.
- **Colour and contrast.** Status is a shape or a ring plus a label, colour only repeats it. The DOM parts (HUD, bars, panels, sheets) are in the contrast sweep per theme; the canvas is not.
- **Sound.** Off by default, one mute control in the HUD, never the only channel.
- **As built in P6a.** The bar is a `role="group"` of buttons, each with an icon and a text label (the icon is a shape and repeats the label); a form is a native `form` with a `fieldset` and a legend, its fields are native `select`, `input` (number, time) and `button` elements with labels, and its refusal is a block announced when it appears. Every text of the bar, the forms, the list, the refusals and the board is in the dictionaries (`src/i18n/*_plant.ts`, `*_act.ts`) under the parity rules; the engine's message of a refusal is shown verbatim in a `lang="en"` element beside the sentence of the reason. The Korean term for the fed-batch feed is 피드 in every Korean dictionary of the lab (section 12, item 15). The turning ring of the recompute line is drawn only when the reader has not asked for reduced motion.
- **As built in P6b.** The flags board is reached without a pointer: its button in the objects list brings the camera to it, and the matrix under it has a button for each cell (a native `button` with an accessible name that says the flag, the hall, its state and what pressing it asks for); the matrix is also a table of words in the text alternative, so a reader without WebGL has the whole board. The cells of the panel differ by shape (raised, flat, a small mark), not by colour alone. The texts are in `src/i18n/*_plant.ts` under the parity rules.
